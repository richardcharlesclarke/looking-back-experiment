## What generative conflict means
Disagreement improves understanding of the problem, understanding of another person's reasoning, or the possibilities available, while preserving the ability to continue the exchange. Agreement, moderation and reduced confidence are not required.
Five outcome families organise reporting: epistemic openness; mutual understanding; generative complexity and new output; relational viability; interactional conduct. The six Study 1 questionnaire domains are narrower self-report constructs mapped into these families, not six competing definitions: humility/curiosity/revisability → openness; opponent regard → relational viability; complexity → generative complexity; agency → perceived capacity for interaction. Study 1 alone does not measure objective mutual understanding or observed conduct.
## Shared topic-change options
Use for E2P_CHANGE, E2A_CHANGE and E3_CHANGE; maximum two responses. “No meaningful change” must be exclusive. Options: No meaningful change; My overall view stayed the same but my reasons became clearer; My view stayed the same but became more qualified; I became less certain; I shifted on one part of the issue; I changed my overall conclusion; I better understood a different view; I left with a new question, distinction or proposal; Something else. Store categories without ranking them as good or bad.
## Treatment briefing — working script
Use the same core script for public panels and pairs, replacing “other participants” with “other panellists” where necessary. Aim for a five-minute slot including private reflection; time it during rehearsal rather than claiming the text already takes five minutes. Delivery medium: [live / recorded video / audio]. Facilitator: [name].
“Before you begin, take a moment to consider how you want to enter this disagreement. The aim of this exercise is to improve the shared understanding of the issue. You do not have to agree, compromise or move towards the middle.
Start with your own position. What is one weakness, uncertainty or circumstance in which it might not hold? Take a moment to identify one privately. [Pause for reflection.]
Next, think of one question about the issue or another person's reasoning whose answer you genuinely do not know. Bring that question into the discussion if the opportunity arises. [Pause for reflection.]
When you challenge another person's point, first try to state the strongest version of what they mean. Where possible, check that you have understood it. Then respond to their reasons. Acknowledging a point does not mean agreeing with it.
If something changes how you see part of the issue, say what you would qualify or add. A qualification can improve the discussion; it does not have to mean abandoning your view.
As you talk, look for a useful distinction, a shared uncertainty, or a conditional proposal that was not clear at the start. You can finish with a clearer disagreement. The aim is to leave with a better account of the problem and of each other's reasons, while remaining able to continue the conversation.”
Do not add improvisational coaching to only some treatment groups. Log script version, medium, duration and deviations. Private reflection is part of the treatment. No requirement to disclose private reflections before baseline, which must already be complete.
## Active-control briefing — working script
Use an equal-duration slot and comparable facilitator contact, filled with neutral logistical information and private review of those logistics. Do not insert listening, perspective-taking, uncertainty or synthesis coaching. Venue-specific facts remain placeholders and can be completed later.
“Before you begin, here are the arrangements for this session. The session is [title or topic] at [location]. It is scheduled to start at [time] and finish at [time]. Your facilitator or moderator is [name].
Please check that you have the participant label or code provided for this session. You will use it to link your questionnaires. Keep your answers private. [Pause to check code.]
The session format is [neutral description of the public panel, or the standard pair timing]. The facilitator will indicate time using [method]. If you need help with the questionnaire or equipment, contact [person/location]. [Pause for logistical questions only.]
After the session, complete the questionnaire using [QR/link/location]. The arrangements for recording and participation are those described in your information sheet. [Review the same logistical information privately for the remainder of the matched briefing slot.]”
Use identical consent, withdrawal and recording information in both conditions before PRE. Equal duration is a target to be timed and logged; blank logistical fields do not prevent implementation of the control condition or instrument routes.
## Neutral facilitator prompts and closing
Study 3: “Your opening time is finished; please let the other person give their opening.” Then: “You now have eight minutes to discuss the issue.” Then: “The discussion time is finished. Please complete the joint record.” Then: “Please complete the final questions privately.” Use the same prompts in both conditions; do not coach participants towards a particular answer.
Public panel: moderator uses the event's agreed format; record moderator identity and any research-specific instructions. Do not give extra treatment coaching through the moderator unless it is specified as part of the intervention for every treatment panel.
Closing: “Thank you for taking part. Please complete any remaining questions privately. You do not need to have reached agreement.” Study explanation/debrief wording: [research team text].
## Briefing fidelity: after main POST in both conditions
- FID_ATTENDED — Did you receive the short briefing before this session? [All; Part; No; Not sure]
- FID_RECALL — What do you remember from the briefing? [Optional text]
- FID_USED — Did anything from the briefing affect how you took part? [No; Yes; Not sure; optional description]
Staff record assigned condition, delivered briefing version, actual duration and deviations. These questions come after outcomes to avoid reminding participants of treatment goals before responding. Keep them out of audience questionnaires.
## Behavioural codebook
Use a speaking turn or identifiable exchange. Store transcript span, speaker label, opportunity flag, code values and short supporting evidence. Hide condition and briefing material from coders; log any accidental disclosure or recognisable cue. Treat blinding as an attempted safeguard, not a guarantee.
- DIRECT_UPTAKE: 0 no engagement with prior contribution; 1 superficial mention/acknowledgement; 2 substantive answer or incorporation. Code opportunity separately. Record whether there was a reasonable opportunity to respond; do not score an opening statement as a failure to engage.
- QUESTION_TYPE: none; rhetorical; clarification; information-seeking; position-testing. Use the dominant purpose with supporting excerpt.
- REPRESENTATION: 0 materially distorted/caricatured; 1 partial or uncertain; 2 accurate/charitable relative to the counterpart's stated reasoning; Not assessable.
- QUALIFICATION: 0 absent; 1 narrows, conditions or revises a claim in response to another contribution.
- DIFFERENTIATION: 0 none; 1 identifies an additional dimension; 2 develops multiple relevant dimensions or trade-offs.
- INTEGRATION: 0 none; 1 relates competing considerations; 2 develops a conditional synthesis or higher-order framing.
- NOVEL_OUTPUT: 0 none; 1 new question or distinction; 2 new framing, mechanism or actionable conditional proposal. “New” means absent from recorded baseline/openings and preceding discussion, not proven new to the world.
- PERSONALISATION, CONTEMPT, EVASION_OR_PARALLEL_TALK: separate 0/1 fields with evidence. Personalisation concerns character/motive attacks rather than challenge to a claim.
- INTERRUPTION: 0/1; optional constructive/destructive/unclear subtype. Do not treat every interruption as harmful.
Retain words or speaking duration for secondary normalised rates. No overall constructive-person or conflict score. Prosodic analysis remains an optional extension; eye contact, posture and warmth do not establish accurate understanding.
Keep the codebook available for reviewing recordings, with a supporting quote or example for each judgement. Record whether coding was human, AI or a combination. If a second reviewer is available, compare a sample to see whether the guidance is clear. Coding capacity can be arranged later.
## Data and interface specification
Core fields: eventId, studyId, participantToken, role, wave, instrumentVersion, itemId, responseValue, missingReason, presentedOrder, startedAt, completedAt, consentVersion. Study-specific: panelId, speakerId, targetSpeakerId, pairId, partnerToken, propositionId, programmeSessionId, assignedCondition, deliveredBriefingVersion, contaminationGroupId, allocationMethod, allocationSeed and exposure timestamps.
A respondent may contribute to several studies; retain one pseudonymous linkage while separating roles/waves. Pair members must not share a response form or see private answers before the defined partner-assessment step. Named public speakers and recordings can be identifiable; do not describe the entire dataset as anonymous merely because it uses tokens.
No default midpoint. Offer labelled seven-point responses for agreement items and an accessible continuous 0–10 scale with numeric alternative for position/confidence/understanding. Keep zero valid; missing is not zero. Prefer not to answer, Cannot assess and Not applicable are distinct missing categories. Exact repeated wording, anchors and IDs remain unchanged across waves.
Retain raw responses and direction metadata. Do not score position moderation, agreement, lower confidence or behavioural preference as inherently better. No respondent personality feedback or total score. Long-form CSV/JSON exports keep contact information separate.
